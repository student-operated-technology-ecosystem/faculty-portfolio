02 · COURSE OUTLINE

CIT-205 Help Desk & User Support

A three-credit applied course that transforms help-desk concepts into professional support practice through the ACE Support Command experiential model inside CCAC's Student-Operated Technology Ecosystem.

Course Design

CIT-205 is organized around a simple progression: LEARN → PRACTICE → OPERATE → QUALIFY. Students begin as new hires, develop support skills through guided practice, apply those skills in increasingly authentic operational work, and culminate in an ACE Qualification Practicum.

The course emphasizes support thinking, customer communication, troubleshooting, ticketing, documentation, process, and professional practice. Technical work is treated as part of a service system rather than as isolated hardware or software exercises.

Curriculum Architecture

The redesigned course maps CCAC course outcomes and established help-desk concepts into ten instructional pillars. Field Briefs teach the concepts required for each stage; ACE and SOTE missions provide the applied work; Blackboard captures the resulting evidence of learning.

Semester operating habit
Handle the work → document the work → improve the system.

Ten-Pillar Learning Sequence

  1. Join the Service Desk
  2. Think Like a Technician
  3. Capture the Incident
  4. Operate the Ticket
  5. Turn Incidents into Knowledge
  6. Control Organizational Knowledge
  7. Operate the Service Platform
  8. Understand the Service Organization
  9. Take Support into the Field
  10. Qualify for Support Operations

Course Outcomes

Course work addresses help-desk roles and responsibilities; customer service; support operations, technologies, and environments; hardware and software needs assessment; product and performance standards; processes, procedures, and service levels; documentation; installation and maintenance responsibilities; and ergonomic factors.

Assessment Strategy

Assessment is distributed across authentic evidence rather than a single examination model. Students demonstrate learning through technical support and problem solving, ticket management and technical documentation, customer service and professional practice, and applied help-desk readiness and qualification.

Representative evidence includes workstation and server validation, troubleshooting scenarios, ticket creation and lifecycle work, knowledge-base development, GitHub documentation workflows, vocabulary and concept checks, service-platform exercises, and the final qualification practicum.

Professional Workflow

The course follows the operational sequence customer → incident → ticket → resolution → knowledge → controlled documentation → better support. GLPI represents service-desk and ticket operations, while GitHub supports controlled documentation and knowledge workflows. Students learn not only to solve a problem, but to preserve what was learned so the organization improves after the incident.

Why This Course Changed

The redesign moves CIT-205 from studying a help desk toward operating within one. Students still encounter the concepts and outcomes required by the course, but they encounter them through authentic support work that produces durable evidence: tickets, documentation, knowledge articles, validation records, troubleshooting decisions, and professional handoffs.
