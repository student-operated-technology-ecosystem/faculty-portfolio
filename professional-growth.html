06 · PROFESSIONAL GROWTH

Professional Growth

My professional growth connects curriculum development, educational research, emerging technology, industry collaboration, and the design of authentic technical learning environments.

Growth Through Research & Scholarship

From 2022–2025, I served as a Co-Principal Investigator on the National Science Foundation Future of Work project A New Bridge to the Digital Economy: Integrated AI-Augmented Learning and Collaboration, a Carnegie Mellon University and CCAC collaboration focused on aligning community-college IT curriculum with AI-driven educational technologies and learning-science frameworks.

This work contributed to the 2024 AERA paper Community College Information Technology Education: Curriculum Mapping, a Learning Science Framework, and AI Learning Technologies, on which I am a named co-author. I was also named as an additional author on 2024 Carnegie Mellon University Teaching & Learning Summit research addressing cross-institutional educational research partnerships and intelligent tutoring systems in community-college IT courses.

Growth Through CEIT Development

My work in the development and launch of CCAC's Center for Education, Innovation & Training expanded my professional practice beyond individual course development into facility planning, stakeholder engagement, industry partnerships, equipment and laboratory strategy, certification programming, and advanced technical learning environments.

CEIT provides a setting in which curriculum, infrastructure, workforce development, and industry expectations intersect. Working across those areas has directly informed how I design technical courses and student experiences.

Industry Certification & Professional Development

I have supported NC3 industry-certification programming and was heavily involved in planning CEIT's first NC3 instructor professional-development / Train-the-Trainer event, held November 13–15, 2023. The three-day program brought more than 34 participants from 21 organizations to the newly opened center.

My certification background also spans CompTIA, Microsoft, Apple, and VMware technologies. These credentials represent a longer pattern of maintaining contact with the technologies, standards, and professional practices that shape the courses I teach.

Growth Through Applied Curriculum Design

The Student-Operated Technology Ecosystem represents the current convergence of this work. I developed SOTE as a framework for turning networking, server, virtualization, cybersecurity, help-desk, storage, and R&D environments into a structured learning ecosystem.

That work has required continued development in infrastructure design, documentation systems, operational workflows, student supervision, knowledge management, and the translation of professional IT practice into assessable learning experiences.

Professional-growth cycle
Learn the technology → build the environment → translate it into curriculum → observe student performance → improve the system.

Impact on Teaching Practice

These experiences have reinforced an applied approach to technical education. Rather than separating professional development from classroom instruction, I use research, infrastructure projects, industry collaboration, and technical experimentation to continuously revise what students encounter in the classroom. Current CIT-205 work demonstrates that progression through authentic ticketing, troubleshooting, documentation, knowledge-management, GitHub, and student-operated support workflows.
