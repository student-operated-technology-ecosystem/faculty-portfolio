03 · SAMPLE ASSIGNMENTS

Applied Technical Missions

Selected CIT-205 assignments ask students to perform, document, and communicate authentic support work. The artifacts are evidence of both technical understanding and professional practice.

Assignment Design

The course uses missions and operational artifacts instead of treating every skill as an isolated worksheet. Students receive a technical situation, determine what must be observed or tested, record defensible evidence, communicate the result, and preserve useful knowledge for the next technician.

Assignment design principle
Do the work → capture the evidence → communicate the result → improve the next response.

ACE-NH-001 · Workstation Validation

Purpose. Introduces students to disciplined technical observation and documentation in a support environment. Students validate a workstation rather than merely identifying components from a diagram.

Student work. Inspect the assigned system, identify and record relevant hardware and configuration information, document findings, and distinguish verified observations from assumptions.

Learning evidence. Hardware recognition, technical vocabulary, systematic inspection, documentation quality, and the ability to create a record another technician can understand and use.

Server Validation Mission

Purpose. Extends workstation-level observation into enterprise-style infrastructure and reinforces safe, methodical work around unfamiliar equipment.

Student work. Work with physical server equipment, open and inspect systems where appropriate, identify components and their functions, record observations, and produce a validation artifact rather than relying on memory or informal discussion.

Learning evidence. Transfer of hardware knowledge, safe technical practice, component identification, team communication, and defensible documentation.

Troubleshooting Scenarios

Purpose. Makes the troubleshooting methodology visible. The assignment evaluates the student's reasoning process, not merely whether a guessed solution happens to work.

Student work. Define the problem, protect the user and organization, develop and test plausible causes safely, document results, determine the next action, and identify when escalation or handoff is appropriate.

Learning evidence. Problem framing, hypothesis development, safe testing, evidence-based decision making, documentation, and professional escalation.

Ticket → Knowledge Base Workflow

Purpose. Connects individual incident resolution to organizational learning. A solved ticket is treated as the beginning of reusable knowledge rather than the end of the exercise.

Student work. Review the incident record, separate case-specific details from reusable information, write a knowledge-base article for an appropriate audience, and structure the article so another technician can locate and apply the resolution.

Learning evidence. Ticket quality, technical writing, audience awareness, knowledge management, repeatability, and the ability to convert operational experience into institutional knowledge.

GitHub Knowledge Workflow

Purpose. Introduces controlled documentation as a professional workflow. Students experience the difference between editing a document privately and contributing to a shared technical knowledge system.

Student work. Work with repository content, branches, proposed changes, review, and contribution workflows while developing or improving technical documentation.

Learning evidence. Version-control vocabulary, collaboration, revision discipline, peer review, change history, and responsibility for shared documentation.

Connection to Course Outcomes

Together, these assignments address help-desk responsibilities, customer and organizational protection, technical support processes, hardware and software evaluation, documentation, standards, procedures, service operations, installation and maintenance responsibilities, and professional communication. They also provide observable artifacts that can be assessed rather than relying solely on students' descriptions of what they understand.

Why These Artifacts Were Selected

The samples show the course's progression from observing a single system, to reasoning through support problems, to documenting incidents, to converting resolutions into reusable knowledge, and finally to maintaining that knowledge through a controlled collaborative workflow. The sequence demonstrates the central instructional goal of CIT-205: students should leave able to participate in a support organization, not simply define one.
