04 · SAMPLE EXAMINATIONS

Assessment Evidence

CIT-205 uses concept checks and applied qualification evidence to assess whether students can recognize support terminology, explain core concepts, and use those concepts in professional technical work.

Assessment Approach

Traditional examinations are one part of the assessment system, not the entire system. Short vocabulary and concept assessments establish whether students understand the language and foundational ideas of help-desk operations. Applied missions then test whether that knowledge transfers into troubleshooting, ticketing, documentation, knowledge management, and professional decision making.

Assessment progression
Recognize the concept → explain the concept → apply the concept → document the evidence.

Vocabulary & Concept Checks

Purpose. Confirm that students can accurately recognize and use the terminology required to participate in technical support work. Vocabulary is assessed because professional communication breaks down quickly when technicians do not share a precise operational language.

What is assessed. Help-desk and service-desk terminology, troubleshooting concepts, ticketing and incident language, hardware and support concepts, documentation and knowledge-management terminology, and vocabulary introduced through the Field Briefs.

Connection to course work. These checks occur alongside applied work so terminology is encountered both as academic content and as language students must use while completing missions and producing artifacts.

Scenario-Based Assessment

Purpose. Evaluate reasoning in situations where simply recalling a definition is insufficient.

What is assessed. Students interpret a support situation, identify relevant information, protect the user and organization, choose safe tests, document evidence, determine an appropriate next action, and recognize when a defensible handoff or escalation is required.

Connection to outcomes. Scenario work provides evidence of troubleshooting methodology, support processes, customer and organizational protection, documentation, professional communication, and decision making.

ACE Qualification Practicum

Purpose. Serve as the culminating assessment of applied help-desk readiness. Rather than asking students only to describe what a technician should do, the practicum requires them to demonstrate the habits developed throughout the course.

What is assessed. Integration of technical observation, troubleshooting, incident and ticket thinking, documentation, knowledge use, communication, workflow discipline, and professional handoff.

Role in the course. The practicum completes the course progression of LEARN → PRACTICE → OPERATE → QUALIFY and provides a final body of evidence showing whether students can combine individual course concepts into support practice.

Rationale for Selected Assessment Evidence

The selected assessments intentionally show more than one level of learning. Vocabulary and concept checks demonstrate foundational understanding. Scenario assessments make student reasoning visible. The qualification practicum demonstrates transfer and integration. Together, they provide stronger evidence of help-desk readiness than a single high-stakes written examination could provide by itself.

Alignment with Course Outcomes

The assessment set supports evaluation of help-desk roles and responsibilities, customer service, support processes and environments, hardware and software considerations, standards and procedures, documentation, installation and maintenance responsibilities, and professional technical practice. The same concepts assessed in written form are revisited in operational assignments so students must both know the material and use it.
